## The build

- **What I built:** A provider profile page for Alex M., a flat-pack furniture assembly specialist serving Central & North Athens, on a marketplace positioning him as "new but verified" with six years of prior experience. The trust panel shows four verification badges (Identity verified, Background check cleared, Insured & certified, Marketplace-verified), plus early social proof in the form of three employer endorsements (a store manager, a senior technician, a property manager) rather than customer star ratings, backed by portfolio photos and a short assembly video. A "Book with confidence" CTA offers 20% off the first booking, alongside marketplace protections (payment held until job confirmation, 14-day fix-it guarantee, free cancellation up to 12 hours prior).
- **Tool used:** Lovable
- **Shareable link:** https://first-booking-boost-sg.lovable.app

## Show & Swap read

_What a partner understood from your build with no verbal setup, their reaction is your first piece of product evidence._

- **What they understood immediately:** A professional profile and booking page for an assembly specialist. It clearly presents their experience, tools and areas of proficiency, alongside recent projects with photos, indicative pricing and time estimates. The profile also builds credibility through endorsements from employers and colleagues, while allowing customers to book the specialist's services directly through the page.
- **What confused them:** It wasn't immediately clear whether this is a broader marketplace where users can browse, compare, and choose between multiple specialists across different services, or whether it is intended to be an individual website dedicated to a single specialist.
- **The assumption they thought you were testing:** The prototype appears to be testing whether giving users comprehensive information in one place builds enough confidence to select a specialist and then convert that decision into a low-friction booking.
- **The gap between what you intended and what they read:** My documented working hypothesis is specifically about a *marketplace* mechanism — helping a brand-new provider earn a first booking and "unlock marketplace liquidity" across many providers. The partner's read landed almost entirely on the *individual provider's* credibility (experience, portfolio, pricing, endorsements) and explicitly could not tell whether a marketplace exists at all behind this page. That's a real gap, not a minor one: the single-provider framing came through clearly, but the multi-provider marketplace context — the actual mechanism the scenario is asking me to prototype — did not read from the page itself.
